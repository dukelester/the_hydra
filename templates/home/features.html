The platform

Features

Follow the money. Find the evidence. Take action.

H.Y.D.R.A. is an evidence-first civic record. Every feature below either helps you find what the files say, watch an area or a project as the record changes, or turn an observation into a structured report. Nothing here scores guilt, corruption, or trust.

Find

Search

Ranked civic search covers projects, institutions, policies, and source documents. Type a project name, county, constituency, contractor, institution, financial year, or words inside an extracted file. Two characters is enough to start. Suggestions appear as you type in the header and on the home page.

Filters can narrow a result set by county, status, and category. A match is a labelled record, not a verdict.

The file

Projects and evidence

A project page holds the civic file: location (county, constituency, ward), institution, contractor, budget, financial year, status, and a timeline from allocation through completion. Stages without a linked source are marked “Evidence unavailable”. The page does not invent a date, an amount, or a finished work.

Evidence coverage counts how many recorded claims are supported by sources. Statuses are verified, partially verified, conflicting information, or unknown. Coverage is not a corruption score, a fraud score, or a trust score.

From the same page you can track the project, favourite it, add it to a comparison, or start an investigation.

The wider record

Institutions, documents, and policies

Institutions are the departments, agencies, and offices named on the file. Opening one shows the projects attached to it.

Documents are the source files — PDFs, spreadsheets, notices — that claims should point to. Where text can be extracted, search can match words inside the file.

Policies are the public rules and plans that sit beside the works. They stay labelled as policies, not as proof that a project was delivered.

Your area

Track my county

After you sign in, choose a county, then optionally a constituency and ward. The lists cascade: Kajiado only offers Kajiado constituencies; a constituency only offers its own wards. You can leave constituency or ward blank to watch the whole county. You can track up to four areas at once.

H.Y.D.R.A. then shows public projects recorded for those areas — status, budget, evidence coverage — and flags records that changed since your last visit. The same feed appears on your dashboard under My areas. Remove one area, or stop all; the location can stay on your profile.

This is a watch on the public record, not a ranking of your county and not a claim that work is complete.

A single project

Track and favourite a project

Track watches one project for a status change or a record update. When the file moves, the dashboard lists the change. Visiting the project clears that alert.

Favourite pins a project you want to keep nearby. It does not subscribe you to alerts. Use it for work you return to often; use Track when you need to notice a change.

Both actions need an account. They stay private to you.

Side by side

Compare projects and counties

Compare puts up to four projects next to each other: county and area, institution, contractor, category, budget, status, and evidence coverage. You can also place counties beside one another using recorded budgets and statuses only.

County highlights show which recorded budget is highest and which county has the highest share of delayed projects in this dataset. Those figures are a reading of the file, not a performance score and not a finding of corruption.

Add a project from its page, or tick projects on the compare screen, then run the comparison.

Your workspace

Dashboard

The dashboard is the signed-in home. It gathers recent views (up to five projects), tracked updates, favourites, your county feed, and recent investigations and reports.

Use it to see what changed since you last looked, then open the project and read the evidence. The dashboard does not publish your work and does not rank you against other users.

What you observed

Investigations

An investigation is a citizen observation attached to a project. You record what you saw, where, and how it compares with the official line. Official information and your observation stay in separate columns. A photo or a note is not treated as verified by default.

Investigations remain private to you unless an administrator reviews them. Language stays descriptive: what was observed, what the file says, and what is still unknown.

Investigate a project

A structured file

Reports

A report is generated from the structured investigation fields. It is a comparison you can review before it is saved, not a conviction. Neutral wording is required: potential discrepancy, evidence indicates, information unavailable, requires further verification.

Each accountability report also shows a visual snapshot of that project’s county: recorded allocation, delivery mix, and sector budgets. Reports stay private by default. They do not determine guilt, corruption, or fraud.

The numbers, drawn

County report

The county report turns the public project file into charts: recorded allocation by county, delivery mix (completed, in progress, delayed), and budgets by sector and financial year. Choose a county to zoom in. Bar length is relative to the largest figure in the set.

These visuals are a reading of recorded allocation and status. They are not paid-out expenditure unless a source records a spend, and they are not a performance or corruption score.

The account

Accounts and privacy

The public record is readable without signing in. An account keeps investigations, reports, tracked projects, favourites, and your county watch with you. You can add a display name, affiliation, role, and area on your profile. Email is optional, but needed to reset a forgotten password.

Username stays yours. Display name is what the dashboard and avatar show. Nothing on the profile is a public score.
