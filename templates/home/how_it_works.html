Method

How it works

From the record to a report — without filling gaps

H.Y.D.R.A. is built around one path: Search → Project → Evidence → Investigation → Report. You can also watch a county, track a project, or compare files along the way. The source of truth stays the evidence. The platform never invents missing facts and never treats an observation as verified by default.

The main path

  1. 1

    Search the record

    Find a project, institution, policy, or document by name, county, constituency, contractor, or words inside a file.

  2. 2

    Open the project

    Read location, budget, status, institution, and the timeline. Stages without a source say evidence is unavailable.

  3. 3

    Read the evidence

    Check claims, source files, and coverage. Coverage measures support in the file. It is not a corruption or trust score.

  4. 4

    Watch, compare, or investigate

    Track the project, favourite it, add it to a comparison, follow your county, or record what you observed.

  5. 5

    Generate a report

    Turn structured investigation fields into a private report. Language stays neutral. The report does not determine wrongdoing.

Evidence first

The source of truth is always the underlying evidence and source documents. H.Y.D.R.A. never invents missing facts, never auto-detects corruption, and never prefers a citizen photo over an official notice — or the other way around.

Official and observed

Official information and citizen observation stay distinct. Both can be recorded. Neither is automatically treated as fact. A gap in the files stays a gap.

The file

Evidence coverage

Coverage measures how many recorded claims are supported by sources. It is not a corruption score, fraud score, or trust score.

  • Verified

    A cited source supports the claim.

  • Partially verified

    The source supports only part of the claim.

  • Conflicting information

    Sources disagree and further verification is required.

  • Unknown

    Evidence is unavailable or insufficient.

Follow

Watch an area or a project

Track my county is for the places you live or follow — up to four areas. Sign in, choose a county, then optionally a constituency and ward. Those lists are linked: a county only shows its own constituencies, and a constituency only shows its own wards. The feed lists public projects for those areas and marks records that changed since your last visit. The same list sits on your dashboard.

Track a project watches one file for a status or record change. Favourite pins a project without the alert. Both stay on the dashboard until you clear them.

Compare

Put up to four projects side by side, or read county totals from the recorded budgets and statuses. Rankings in this view are a reading of the file — highest recorded budget, highest delayed share — not a performance or corruption score.

Structured file

Reports

Reports are generated from structured investigation fields. They remain private by default. Language stays neutral: potential discrepancy, evidence indicates, information unavailable, requires further verification.

The county report adds charts for recorded allocation, delivery status, and sector mix across counties. An accountability report includes a snapshot of the project’s county so the file can be read as numbers as well as text.
