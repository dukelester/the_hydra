About Hydra

Human-centered Yield, Data, Rights & Accountability

H.Y.D.R.A. is an evidence-first civic record for public projects, budgets, institutions, policies, and source documents. It helps people follow the money, watch a county or a project as the file changes, compare what the record shows, and take action — without turning a gap in the files into a finding of guilt.

Mission

Keep the public record inspectable

H.Y.D.R.A. exists to hold an evidence-first civic record of public projects, budgets, institutions, policies, and source documents. We help people follow the money, see what the files actually support, and take action — without inventing missing facts or turning a gap in the record into a finding of guilt.

Vision

One labelled record, many arms of scrutiny

We work toward a civic landscape where any public project can be inspected from more than one direction. Residents, journalists, researchers, and oversight bodies should share the same labelled file: missing information stays missing, a citizen observation stays an observation, and accountability has a clear path from evidence to report.

Why this name

The name is an acronym, not a verdict. Each letter is a principle the platform is built to keep in view: people first, results over announcements, verifiable information, the right to know, and a path from evidence to oversight.

It was chosen because it describes the work without implying corruption. A missing document is recorded as missing. A citizen observation stays an observation. A report stays a structured comparison, not a conviction.

H

Human-centered

Citizens are at the heart of the platform. H.Y.D.R.A. is built for people who need to inspect public work — residents, journalists, researchers, and oversight bodies — not for institutions to speak over them.

An account keeps your investigations and reports with you. The public record remains readable without signing in. You should never have to invent a story to make a page look complete.

Y

Yield

Yield asks whether public money and policies actually deliver results. An allocation in a budget is not the same as a finished classroom, a working borehole, or a completed road.

Project pages keep status, contractor, financial year, and timeline stages only as far as the record goes. If a stage has no evidence, the page says evidence is unavailable — it does not fill the gap.

D

Data

Data here means verifiable public information: project records, budget figures, policies, and source files such as PDFs, spreadsheets, and notices. Search can match a name, a county, a contractor, a filename, or words inside an extracted document.

Every claim should point to a source and a verification status. Evidence coverage measures how much of the recorded information is supported. It is not a corruption score, a fraud score, or a trust score.

R

Rights

The right to know what was budgeted, who is responsible, and what the attached files actually say is the starting point for action. H.Y.D.R.A. is meant to make that information easier to find and harder to blur.

It does not replace access-to-information law, a court, or an auditor. It holds the public record in one place so people can use those other channels with a clearer file.

A

Accountability

Accountability on this platform means a path: search a project, read the evidence, watch your area or a file as it is updated, record what you observed, and generate a structured report. Official information and citizen observation stay in separate columns so one cannot quietly overwrite the other.

Reports remain private by default. Language stays neutral — potential discrepancy, evidence indicates, information unavailable, requires further verification. The report does not determine wrongdoing.

What H.Y.D.R.A. does

  • Publishes labelled civic records for projects, institutions, policies, and source documents.
  • Shows budget, status, county, constituency, ward, and evidence coverage only as far as sources support them.
  • Lets you search names, places, contractors, and text inside uploaded files.
  • Track my county: follow a county, constituency, or ward and see projects as the record is updated.
  • Lets you track or favourite a project, and compare projects or counties side by side.
  • Keeps a citizen observation distinct from the official record.
  • Turns an investigation into a structured report you can review before anything is saved.

What it does not do

  • It does not determine guilt, corruption, or fraud.
  • It does not invent missing facts or complete a timeline the files do not support.
  • It does not treat a photo, a rumour, or an observation as verified by default.
  • It does not score people, counties, or contractors for trust. Compare totals and county rankings are readings of the file, not a performance verdict.
  • It does not publish your investigation or report unless you take the next step, or an administrator reviews it.

The emblem

The circular mark is original to this platform: a map of Africa, including Madagascar, held by gold tentacles on teal. Many arms, one record — public work should be inspectable from more than one direction, and no single missing page should erase the rest of the file.

It is not a copy of a comic-book hydra. There is no skull and no claim of conspiracy. The image is a civic seal: continent at the centre, evidence reaching around it.

Who it is for

Anyone who needs a clear view of a public project: a resident checking a local clinic, a student tracing a bursary, a journalist confirming a contractor name, or an official comparing what was allocated with what was built.

Some records on the site are labelled [DEMO]. Those are fictional examples for learning the interface. They are not live public data and must not be quoted as fact.

How to use it

  1. 1

    Search the record

    Find a project, institution, policy, or document by name, county, constituency, or words inside a file.

  2. 2

    Read the evidence

    Open claims, source files, coverage, and notes that say when information is unavailable.

  3. 3

    Watch and compare

    Track your county, follow a project, or put files side by side. The dashboard flags updates since you last looked.

  4. 4

    Investigate and report

    Record what you observed, compare it with the official line, and generate a structured report.
